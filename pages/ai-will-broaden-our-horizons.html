AI Will Broaden Our Horizons

TL;DR

  1. With the help of AI, technical people can now create software they could previously only imagine.
  2. Technical people often have more room to think creatively and outside the box than developers traditionally have.
  3. Character training can help everyone think outside the box and feel confident enough to reinvent the world.

The title might seem obvious. Everyone knows that AI enables people to create software they could never have imagined building before.

But that's not what I want to talk about right now. There's something else happening, something whose effects many people can feel without necessarily understanding why they're starting to think differently.

For decades, coming up with fundamentally new ways to engineer things often required either a fairly large team or exceptional talent. I'm not talking about finding a faster or more elegant solution to an existing problem, but about rethinking the problem itself and approaching it in a completely different way.

Most people never considered reimagining the things we take for granted. Others had ideas but lacked the skills or resources to turn them into working software. The people who managed to think differently and bring their ideas to life were the ones who pushed the field forward. Thanks to them, the engineering world has become what it is today.

Now, with the help of AI, many people who previously had great ideas but couldn't implement them can finally bring those ideas to life. But that's only the first part of the story. It lays the groundwork for what I really want to explore.

So, how did I come up with these thoughts? It started with a YouTube video by Nathan Lambert, in which he discussed shaping the character of an AI model and how that influences the way humans perceive and interact with it. The second video was one I watched today, and it motivated me to write this post.

In that video, Theo talks about how technical people who may have had great ideas for years but lacked the ability to implement them can now actually build them, just as I described above. His argument is that developers need to become more creative and think outside the box, while the technology itself needs to improve to make even more ambitious ideas possible.

My first thought was to combine these two ideas. It's not just that models need to become smarter to empower creative people, nor is it just that developers need to become more creative. Both perspectives leave out what I believe could be the most important element in this process: the AI itself. In these views, AI is treated primarily as a tool that writes the software. But what if it could do much more than that?

From Theo's other videos, I know that he tries to involve AI agents as early as possible in the development process and to plan as much as possible together with them. This is where character training becomes especially interesting.

The character of a model influences how it approaches problems, the kinds of suggestions it makes, the values it reflects, and how it motivates the people working with it. During the planning phase, these qualities can make the difference between exploring entirely new possibilities and getting stuck in the same familiar patterns of thinking.

This is where the future begins to take shape. It's not primarily about how much knowledge a model has or whether it can produce a correct answer to a particular question. Those things matter, of course. But there's another dimension worth paying attention to: the model's tendency to explore, challenge assumptions, suggest alternatives, and encourage us to aim higher. Reinforcement learning (RL), including the choices made when shaping a model's character, can play an important role in developing these tendencies.

Imagine working with an AI that doesn't just help you implement your ideas, but actively encourages you to question your assumptions, explore unconventional approaches, and imagine things you would never have considered building before. Such a model doesn't simply make you more productive. It can change the way you think about what's possible in the first place.

That's the point I want to make: we should appreciate not only when a model becomes more capable, but also when it becomes more enjoyable and inspiring to work with. The way a model communicates, challenges us, and encourages us to explore new ideas can have a profound effect on what we choose to create.

And perhaps this is one reason why we'll see a sudden leap in the kinds of things developers attempt to build. It won't just be because AI has become smarter or because people can write software more easily. It will also be because working with AI changes how people think about what is possible. When the tool itself helps us think beyond our current limitations, our horizons expand.
