Why I Started This Website

For the past month I've been coding mostly with Claude Code. I'm more productive than I've ever been and have been able to build more than I thought possible. But there have also been downsides. One of them is the reason this website exists.

When you hand over most of what you used to do as a software developer to a clanker, you suddenly have more time to think about projects, architecture, and design decisions. At least, that's the idea. I believe that's only true under certain conditions.

The reason it's better to write down your thoughts than to record your voice is that writing lets you restructure and process your ideas, because you're able to edit and refine what you've written. This is especially true when writing by hand. I believe, the same is true for writing code.

When you solve a new problem yourself, you can draw from experience, but you still have to think through every step. The time you spend writing-and rewriting-code isn't wasted. It's the time during which your ideas become precise. Writing code is a way of formalizing thought.

Now, with the rise of clankers, you can hand all of that work to an agent and only think about the end result you want to produce. It's incredibly powerful, but it also removes the valuable time that used to exist between the initial idea and the finished project.

I know that I miss that time myself. At the same time, I want to build more projects and explore new ideas. Would I learn more by writing everything myself? Absolutely. Would I build far fewer things in the same amount of time? Also yes.

What exactly you write about doesn't matter nearly as much as the act of writing itself. Writing forces your brain to reorganize ideas, expose weak assumptions, and create new connections. If you don't know what to write, just begin. The ideas will come.

That's why I'm writing on this website. Thoughts only grow when you give them space, just like a tree whose growth is limited by the size of the pot that holds its roots.
